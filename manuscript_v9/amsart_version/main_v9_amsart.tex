\begin{abstract}
We certify reduced and neural approximations of European-put price trajectories under the
Carr--Geman--Madan--Yor model by residual bounds for the spline Galerkin discretisation of the
tempered-fractional pricing equation.  The Galerkin matrices are Toeplitz sections of an explicit
periodised Fourier symbol; summing the alias series to a stated tail makes the assembled system
the exact Galerkin system to $10^{-8}$.  A L\'evy--Khintchine argument gives the parameter-explicit
lower bound $\lambda_{\min}(S_I)\ge\min_\vartheta s_\mu(\vartheta)-\varepsilon_K\ge r\,m_{\min}$ for
the symmetric part, valid for $Y\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}$, which replaces numerical spectral estimates
and positivity audits.  A banded comparison norm built from the mass and stiffness symbols yields a
rigorous certificate at $O(N)$ cost with explicit effectivity, and empirical interpolation of the
symbol family makes the operator affine in about 36 terms with a rigorous error transfer, so that a
reduced model is certified at a cost independent of $N$; a three-stage rule escalates to the
full-order bounds only when needed.  At the resolution that prices the whole parameter box to one
cent ($N=1024$), the full-order certificate costs $6\%$ of a direct solve, the certified
POD--Galerkin model of rank $80$ costs $3\%$ of it and settles $99.8\%$ of $10^4$ queries without
touching the full space.  No false accept occurs on any query with a reference solution (150 in
the parameter box, 264 outside it), and outside the box the certificate accepts 193 of the 210
queries on which the reduced model is still within one cent.  The neural
reduced models trained here fail the one-cent tolerance by a factor of at least $46$; the certificate
detects this at every query for a few milliseconds and explains it as a trade-off between
temporal smoothness and coefficient accuracy.  A residual certificate for the American put
in the same framework is proved as an outlook.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
The CGMY tempered-stable process models asymmetric jumps and small-jump
activity through four parameters \cite{CarrGemanMadanYor2002}. European
pricing under this model leads to a nonlocal, nonsymmetric parabolic PIDE
whose fractional order varies with the model parameters
\cite{CarteadelCastilloNegrete2007}. Repeated pricing, calibration and
uncertainty quantification motivate approximations of the parameter-to-price
map. An aggregate prediction error, however, provides no error bound for an
individual query outside well-represented regions of the training set.

Neural solution maps, including DeepONet \cite{LuJinPangZhangKarniadakis2021}
and Fourier neural operators \cite{LiKovachkiEtAl2021,KovachkiEtAl2023},
provide expressive surrogate models. Nonlocal kernel networks explicitly
incorporate nonlocal structure \cite{YouYuDEliaGaoSilling2022}, while neural
reduced models learn coordinates in a prescribed trial space
\cite{HesthavenUbbiali2018,BhattacharyaEtAl2021}. Query-wise residual bounds
originate in certified reduced-basis methods for parametric evolution
problems \cite{GreplPatera2005,RozzaHuynhPatera2008,HaasdonkOhlberger2008}
and option pricing \cite{ContLantosPironneau2011}. Variationally correct
operator learning emphasises that the residual norm must reflect stability
of the underlying problem \cite{QiuDahmenChen2026}.

This paper combines these ideas for CGMY pricing with a spline-valued
surrogate. The deterministic discretisation defines the coefficient space,
training trajectories, residual and correction operator. Established
finite-difference, Galerkin and transform methods supply the wider numerical
context \cite{ContVoltchkova2005,MatacheVonPetersdorffSchwab2004,
HilberReichmannSchwabWinter2013,FangOosterlee2008}; the spline construction
follows \cite{DamircheliCGMY2026}. The specific analytical features are the
parameter-dependent energy order, the nonsymmetric fractional operator,
and the restriction to free coefficients after imposing exterior data.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item exact assembly of the spline Galerkin matrices from the periodised
Fourier symbol with an explicit truncation bound (Lemmas~\ref{lem:periodisation}--\ref{lem:tail});
\item a rigorous, parameter-explicit spectral lower bound for the free symmetric part
(Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}), valid for $Y\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}$, replacing the
numerical spectral estimate and the positivity audit of the earlier version;
\item a banded comparison norm giving an $O(N)$ residual certificate with explicit effectivity,
and a two-stage rule with a circulant-preconditioned exact Riesz solve
(Theorem~\ref{thm:comparison}, Proposition~\ref{prop:twostage});
\item an affine decomposition of the symbol family with rigorous error transfer, hence an
$N$-independent certified reduced model (Lemma~\ref{lem:eim}, Theorem~\ref{thm:eim-cert});
\item matched evaluations at the resolution that prices the parameter box to one cent: the cost
of the certificate at full and reduced order, a certified reduced model an order of magnitude
faster than the direct solve, out-of-distribution detection, and a negative result for the neural
reduced models that the certificate itself establishes and explains.
\end{enumerate}
The discrete price is compared with the COS reference \cite{FangOosterlee2008} throughout, so that
the certified trajectory bound is a stated part of a one-cent price budget rather than a substitute
for it.
The principal computational finding is that the guarantee is cheap and the reduced model
earns it: at $N=1024$ the full-order certificate costs $6\%$ of the direct solve, the certified
reduced model of rank $80$ runs at $3\%$ of it with $99.8\%$ of $10^4$ queries settled at the
$N$-independent stage, while the neural reduced models trained here fail the tolerance by a
factor of at least $46$ and are rejected at every query for a few milliseconds.

Query-wise bounds for parametric evolution problems are the subject of the certified
reduced-basis literature \cite{GreplPatera2005,RozzaHuynhPatera2008,HaasdonkOhlberger2008},
where the coercivity constant is obtained by the successive constraint method
\cite{HuynhRozzaSenPatera2007} and non-affine operators by empirical interpolation
\cite{BarraultMadayNguyenPatera2004,GreplMadayNguyenPatera2007}; option-pricing applications
include \cite{ContLantosPironneau2011,BurkovskaHaasdonkSalomonWohlmuth2015} and parametric
Chebyshev interpolation of prices \cite{GassGlauMahlstedtMair2018}.  What is specific here is that
the discretisation is shift-invariant, so that the symbol calculus of Toeplitz sections
\cite{GrenanderSzego1958,BottcherSilbermann1999} replaces the successive constraint method by a
closed-form constant, the interpolation acts on a function of one variable, and the circulant
preconditioner of Chan and Strang \cite{ChanStrang1989} makes the exact Riesz solve a dozen
iterations at any resolution.

The analysis distinguishes the assembled discrete system from the exact
Galerkin discretisation and the continuum price. Residual certification
controls distance to the exact solution of the first. The variational
results describe the second under exact assembly, and Fourier comparisons
assess the third empirically. The numerical implementation uses ordinary
floating point; its arithmetic assumptions are stated with the bracket.

\section{CGMY model and constrained discretisation}
\label{sec:model}\label{sec:galerkin}
\subsection{CGMY backward operator}
\label{ssec:operator}

Let $S_t=e^{X_t}$ under a risk-neutral measure and let $V(x,\tau)$ be the
option value in the log-price variable $x=\log S$ and backward time
$\tau=T-t$.  Following the convention of the companion paper
\cite{DamircheliCGMY2026}, the CGMY parameters satisfy
\begin{equation}
  C>0,\qquad G>0,\qquad M>1,\qquad Y\in(0,2),\qquad Y\ne1,
  \label{eq:cgmy-parameters}
\end{equation}
where $M>1$ is the exponential-moment condition that makes the stock
martingale correction
\begin{equation}
  \omega_S(\mu)
  = C\Gamma(-Y)\bigl[(M-1)^Y-M^Y+(G+1)^Y-G^Y\bigr]
  \label{eq:martingale-correction}
\end{equation}
real valued.  Set
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_0(\mu) := \rf + C\Gamma(-Y)\bigl(M^Y+G^Y\bigr).
  \label{eq:lambda0}
\end{equation}
For $Y\in(1,2)$ one has $\Gamma(-Y)>0$ and hence $\lambda_0>\rf$.  The
tempered left- and right-sided Riemann--Liouville operators are
\begin{equation}
  \Dm u=e^{-Gx}\,{}_{-\infty}D_x^Y\bigl(e^{Gx}u\bigr),
  \qquad
  \Dp u=e^{Mx}\,{}_{x}D_\infty^Y\bigl(e^{-Mx}u\bigr),
  \label{eq:tempered-ops}
\end{equation}
and the single backward-time operator used for both the theory and the
discretisation is
\begin{equation}
  \Aop u
  =-(\rf-\omega_S)u_x
   -C\Gamma(-Y)\Dp u
   -C\Gamma(-Y)\Dm u
   +\lambda_0u.
  \label{eq:cgmy-operator}
\end{equation}
The European pricing problem is
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\tau V+\Aop V=0,
  \qquad
  V(\cdot,0)=\psi.
  \label{eq:pricing-pide}
\end{equation}
On the full line the Fourier symbols are
$\widehat{\Dm u}(\xi)=(G+i\xi)^Y\widehat u(\xi)$ and
$\widehat{\Dp u}(\xi)=(M-i\xi)^Y\widehat u(\xi)$ on the principal branch, so
that
\begin{equation}
  A_\mu(\xi)
  =-(\rf-\omega_S)i\xi
   -C\Gamma(-Y)\bigl[(M-i\xi)^Y+(G+i\xi)^Y\bigr]
   +\lambda_0 .
  \label{eq:symbol}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Parameter family and exterior data}
\label{ssec:family}\label{ssec:finite-domain}
The coefficient parameters lie in the compact interior box
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Pset=\bigl\{\mu=(C,G,M,Y,\rf):\;&
 C\in[C_{\min},C_{\max}],\quad C_{\min}>0,\\
 &G\in[G_{\min},G_{\max}],\quad G_{\min}>0,\\
 &M\in[M_{\min},M_{\max}],\quad M_{\min}>1,\\
 &Y\in[Y_{\min},Y_{\max}]\Subset(1,2),\\
 &\rf\in[r_{\min},r_{\max}],\quad r_{\min}>0\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:parameter-set}
\end{equation}

Maturity $T$ is an additional surrogate input determining the time horizon;
$\psi$ is the payoff. The numerical parameter ranges are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:proposed-box}.

The restrictions $C_{\min}>0$, $r_{\min}>0$ and
$[Y_{\min},Y_{\max}]\Subset(1,2)$ give uniform coercivity.
The condition $M_{\min}>1$ selects the interior exponential-moment regime.
A common truncated domain $\Omega=(x_{\min},x_{\max})$ is used throughout
the family.

Nonlocal exterior values enter through $V=g+U$, with $U$ extended by zero
outside $\Omega$. For a put, a smooth lifting is
$g(x,\tau)=(\mathcal Ke^{-r\tau}-e^x)\zeta(x)$, where $\zeta$ equals one to the left
of the domain and zero to its right. With $s_\mu=Y/2$, define
\begin{equation}
  \Vmu=\Hs(\Omega)
  =\bigl\{v\in L^2(\Omega):\widetilde v\in H^{s_\mu}(\R)\bigr\},
  \qquad
  \norm{v}_{\Vmu}:=\norm{\widetilde v}_{H^{s_\mu}_{M,G}(\R)},
  \label{eq:energy-space}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde v$ is extension by zero and the full-line norm is defined by
\begin{equation}
  \norm{u}_{H^{q}_{M,G}(\R)}^2
  :=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R
  \Bigl(1+|M-i\xi|^{2q}+|G+i\xi|^{2q}\Bigr)|\widehat u(\xi)|^2\,d\xi .
  \label{eq:HMG-norm}
\end{equation}
The finite-domain bilinear form is
$a_\mu(u,w):=\langle\Aop\widetilde u,\widetilde w\rangle$, and the truncated
weak problem is: find $U\in L^2(0,T;\Vmu)$ with
$\partial_\tau U\in L^2(0,T;\Vmu')$, $U(\cdot,0)=\psi-g(\cdot,0)$, and
\begin{equation}
  (\partial_\tau U,w)_{L^2(\Omega)}+a_\mu(U,w)=\ell^g(w;\tau)
  \qquad\forall\,w\in\Vmu,
  \label{eq:weak-problem}
\end{equation}
where the lifting functional is
\begin{equation}
  \ell^{g}(w;\tau)
  :=-\int_\Omega\partial_\tau g\,w\,dx-\int_\Omega(\Aop g)\,w\,dx .
  \label{eq:lifting-functional}
\end{equation}
All exterior data enter only through $\Aop g$ evaluated on $\Omega$.


The put payoff is $\psi(x)=(\mathcal K-e^x)^+$ with strike $\mathcal K$ (the letter $K$ is reserved
for the alias truncation of Section~\ref{ssec:assembly}). The main study fixes $\mathcal K=100$ and
learns variation in $(\mu,T)$; this is a parameter-to-trajectory map within
one payoff family.

\subsection{Uniform variational stability}
\label{ssec:uniform}
\begin{theorem}[Parameter-uniform continuity and coercivity]
\label{thm:uniform-coercivity}
For the family \eqref{eq:parameter-set}, there are constants
$C_{\rm cont}<\infty$ and $c_1>0$, independent of $\mu$, such that
\begin{equation}
 |a_\mu(v,w)|\le C_{\rm cont}\|v\|_{V_\mu}\|w\|_{V_\mu},
 \qquad a_\mu(v,v)\ge c_1\|v\|_{V_\mu}^2
 \quad(v,w\in V_\mu).
 \label{eq:uniform-coercivity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Explicit choices and the proof appear in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-uniform}.
\end{proof}
The constants establish uniform stability despite the varying energy order.
They are conservative; the discrete bounds of Section~\ref{ssec:spectral} are the ones used
in the certificate.  The continuum statement keeps the hypothesis $Y>1$: for $Y<1$ the drift
term is not controlled by the $H^{Y/2}_{M,G}$ norm.  The discrete results below hold for
$Y\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}$.

\subsection{Spline space and assembly}
\label{ssec:spline-space}
Let $N_p$ be the cardinal spline of order $p$ (degree $p-1$),
$h=(x_{\max}-x_{\min})/N$, and
$\phi_{h,k}(x)=h^{-1/2}N_p((x-x_{\min})/h-k)$. The homogeneous space is
\begin{equation}
 V_h^0=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{h,k}:k=0,\ldots,N-p\},
 \qquad N_h=N-p+1.
 \label{eq:Vh}
\end{equation}
For $p\ge2$, these interior-supported splines have zero extensions in
$H^1(\R)$ and hence in $V_\mu$ for $Y/2<1$. Exact variational assembly gives
\begin{equation}
 (\Mh)_{kk'}=(\phi_{h,k'},\phi_{h,k})_{L^2(\Omega)},
 \qquad
 (\Ah(\mu))_{kk'}=a_\mu(\phi_{h,k'},\phi_{h,k}),
 \label{eq:matrices}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
 \Ah(\mu)
 =-(\rf-\omega_S)D_h
  -C\Gamma(-Y)T_M^+
  -C\Gamma(-Y)T_G^-
  +\lambda_0\Mh,
 \label{eq:Ah}
\end{equation}
where $D_h$ is the first-derivative matrix and the fractional blocks are
Toeplitz with the $h^{-Y}$ scaling
\begin{equation}
 (T_G^-)_{kk'}=h^{-Y}\Lambda_G^-(k-k';Gh),
 \qquad
 (T_M^+)_{kk'}=h^{-Y}\Lambda_M^+(k-k';Mh),
 \label{eq:toeplitz-blocks}
\end{equation}
the tempered connection coefficients $\Lambda_G^-,\Lambda_M^+$ being the absolutely convergent
Fourier-symbol integrals made explicit in Section~\ref{ssec:assembly}.  The matrices $\Mh$ and $D_h$
are banded; the Toeplitz blocks are applied by circulant embedding in
$O(N_h\log N_h)$ operations with no compression error.  The semidiscrete
system is
\begin{equation}
  \Mh\dot\cc(\tau)+\Ah(\mu)\cc(\tau)=\ff(\tau;\mu,\psi),
  \qquad
  f_k(\tau)=\ell^{g}(\phi_{h,k};\tau).
  \label{eq:semidiscrete-system}
\end{equation}


\subsection{Exact assembly from the periodised symbol}
\label{ssec:assembly}
Every matrix in \eqref{eq:Ah} is the Galerkin matrix of a Fourier multiplier in a shift-invariant
basis, and is therefore a Toeplitz matrix whose entries are the Fourier coefficients of a
$2\pi$-periodic function, the \emph{periodised symbol}.  This observation replaces quadrature of
the connection coefficients by an alias sum with an explicit tail.
\input{theory/lem_periodisation}
\input{theory/lem_tail}
The series \eqref{eq:periodised} is evaluated on the grid $\vartheta_j=-\pi+2\pi j/L$ with
$L\ge 2N_h$ a multiple of $N_h$; the $2N_h-1$ Toeplitz entries then follow from one inverse FFT,
$t_m=(-1)^m\,[\mathcal F_L^{-1}\sym{a}_{\mu,K}]_{m\bmod L}$, with an aliasing error
$\sum_{j\ne0}|t_{m+jL}|$ that decays like $e^{-\min(G,M)h\,(L-N_h)}$ because the fractional symbol is
analytic in the strip $|\Im\vartheta|<\min(G,M)h$; with $L=4N_h$ the exponent is at least $46$ on the
box.  The cost is $O(LK)$ scalar operations per query, independent of the certificate.
All experiments use $K=100$ with the exact trigonometric polynomials for the mass, drift and
stiffness parts; then $\varepsilon_K(\mu)\le5\times10^{-9}\,\norm{\Ah}_2$ on the box of
Table~\ref{tab:proposed-box} (Table~\ref{tab:assembly-v9}), and $\varepsilon_K$ is recorded with
every assembled operator because it enters the certificate.  The principal-period rule of the
earlier version of this work, which samples only the $k=0$ term, is the case $K=0$; its relative
operator-norm error is $0.31$--$0.41$ at every resolution because at $\vartheta=\pm\pi$ the $k=0$
and $k=\mp1$ terms of \eqref{eq:periodised} have equal modulus, so the error does not decrease
under refinement.  With $K\ge100$ the assembled matrices are the exact Galerkin matrices to
$10^{-8}$, and every exact-assembly statement applies to them with the explicit perturbation
\eqref{eq:tail}.  The correctness of \eqref{eq:periodised} is checked independently in
Section~\ref{ssec:e0-assembly} against entries computed at 40 digits.

The algebraic facts about Toeplitz sections that the certificate uses are classical
\cite{GrenanderSzego1958,BottcherSilbermann1999}; we record them with proofs because the
constants matter.

\subsection{Prescribed coefficients and the free system}
\label{ssec:constrained-system}
Write $\bar M,\bar A$ for the raw $(N+p-1)$-dimensional assembled matrices,
and let $E$ inject the $N-p+1$ free coefficients into the raw vector.
Let $b^n$ vanish on the free entries and carry the prescribed entries on the
boundary. Every admissible trajectory has the form
\[
 c^n=Ez^n+b^n,\qquad
 M_I=E^\top\bar M E,\quad A_I=E^\top\bar A E,\quad
 S_I=\tfrac12(A_I+A_I^\top)=E^\top\bar S E,
 \quad \bar S=\tfrac12(\bar A+\bar A^\top).
\]
For the implementation, the left prescribed coefficients equal
$\mathcal K e^{-r\tau_n}\sqrt h$ and the right ones equal zero. These are
coefficient constraints; they are not four extra PIDE equations.
Restricting the CN equations to the free rows gives
\begin{equation}
 M_I\frac{z^{n+1}-z^n}{\Delta\tau}
 +A_I\frac{z^{n+1}+z^n}{2}=f_I^{n+1/2},\qquad
 f_I^{n+1/2}=E^\top\!\left[\bar f^{n+1/2}
 -\bar M\frac{b^{n+1}-b^n}{\Delta\tau}
 -\bar A\frac{b^{n+1}+b^n}{2}\right].
 \label{eq:constrained-cn}
\end{equation}
Even when the stored raw forcing $\bar f$ is zero, the effective free forcing
generally is not. For a candidate with the identical $b^n$, its residual is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_I^{n+1/2}=E^\top\!\left[\bar M\frac{c_\theta^{n+1}-c_\theta^n}{\Delta\tau}
 +\bar A\frac{c_\theta^{n+1}+c_\theta^n}{2}-\bar f^{n+1/2}\right].
 \label{eq:constrained-residual}
\end{equation}
Thus extracting the free rows of the raw residual is exactly equivalent to
forming the lifted free-space residual. Boundary discrepancies must first be
removed by projection or separately bounded; zeroing their residual entries
alone does not control them. The error then satisfies $e^n=Ee_I^n$ and
$(e^n)^\top\bar M e^n=(e_I^n)^\top M_I e_I^n$.
Because the free indices are contiguous, the free matrices are Toeplitz sections of the
symbols of Lemma~\ref{lem:periodisation}:
\begin{equation}
 A_I=\Tn{N_h}{\sym{a}_\mu},\qquad M_I=\Tn{N_h}{\sym{m}},\qquad
 S_I=\Tn{N_h}{\sym{s}_\mu},\quad \sym{s}_\mu:=\Real\sym{a}_\mu,\qquad
 h^2R^{(1)}_I=\Tn{N_h}{\sym{g}_1},
 \label{eq:sections}
\end{equation}
the drift symbol being odd and therefore absent from the symmetric part; $h^2R^{(1)}_I$ is banded with
bandwidth $p-1$.  The raw matrices $\bar M,\bar A$ are the sections $\Tn{N+p-1}{\sym{m}},\Tn{N+p-1}{\sym{a}_\mu}$
of the full cardinal splines, so the prescribed boundary functions are not truncated to $\Omega$
in the lifting term; for the mass and drift couplings this coincides with quadrature over $\Omega$,
and the initial projection $c^0_h$ uses the $\Omega$-quadrature mass matrix so that constants are
reproduced exactly up to the boundary.
The reliability, efficiency and inexact-Riesz results below apply directly to
$(M_I,A_I,S_I,\rho_I)$; positivity of $S_I$ is now a theorem (Section~\ref{ssec:spectral}). 
The raw basis has indices $k=-(p-1),\ldots,N-1$. Prescribing the first and
last $p-1$ coefficients leaves exactly \eqref{eq:Vh}. At $N=64$, order
$p=3$ therefore means quadratic splines, 66 stored coefficients and 62 free
unknowns. Certification uses $M_I$ itself, rather than a restriction of
$\bar M^{-1}$, and restricts all operator and preconditioner actions to the
same free space.

\begin{remark}[Assembled versus exact sections]\label{rem:compression}
The matrices actually assembled are the sections built from the $L$-point DFT coefficients of
$\sym{s}_{\mu,K}$ (Section~\ref{ssec:assembly}).  Up to the diagonal unitary similarity
$\operatorname{diag}((-1)^k)$, such a section is the leading $N_h\times N_h$ compression of the
$L\times L$ circulant whose eigenvalues are the samples $\sym{s}_{\mu,K}(\vartheta_j)$, so for the
assembled matrix $\lambda_{\min}(S_I)\ge\min_j\sym{s}_{\mu,K}(\vartheta_j)$ holds \emph{exactly},
and likewise $\norm{\Tn{N_h}{f}-\Tn{N_h}{g}}_2\le\max_j|f(\vartheta_j)-g(\vartheta_j)|$ for two
symbols sampled on the same grid.  The derivative margin of the grid minimum and the tail
$\varepsilon_K$ are needed only to relate the assembled system to the exact Galerkin matrix; the
implementation keeps both, so that $\alpha_{\rm lb}$ is valid for either matrix.
\end{remark}
Over 32 box vertices and 256 Sobol points at $N=64$, $256$ and $1024$,
$\lambda_{\min}(S_I)/\smin(\mu)\in[1.003,17.8]$ and $\lambda_{\min}(S_I)/(\rf m_{\min})\in[7.5,133]$
(Figure~\ref{fig:spectral-bound}), the ratio closest to one being attained at the worst vertex of
the earlier positivity audit ($\mu=(0.05,5,9,1.1,0.01)$, $\lambda_{\min}=0.01038$, $\smin=\rf=0.0100$).
$\smin(\mu)$ is the minimum of an explicit smooth even function of one variable.  It is
evaluated on a graded grid (a uniform grid of $2^{14}$ points plus a geometric refinement on the
scale $\min(G,M)h$ near $\vartheta=0$, where the fractional part of the symbol varies fastest)
and shifted outward by a cell-wise derivative margin $\tfrac12 w\,\sup_{\rm cell}|\sym{s}'_{\mu}|$, the
derivative being evaluated from the explicit formula
$\partial_\vartheta(\delta+i\vartheta)^Y=iY(\delta+i\vartheta)^{Y-1}$.  The only step that is not an
enclosure is that $\sup_{\rm cell}|\sym{s}'_\mu|$ is taken as the larger endpoint value; a validated
implementation would evaluate $\sym{s}'_\mu$ in interval arithmetic on each cell.  On the box the
minimum is attained at $\vartheta=0$, where $\sym{s}_\mu(0)=\rf$ exactly, so in practice
$\alpha_{\rm lb}=\rf-\varepsilon_K(\mu)$ and the margin is of the order $10^{-8}$.
Since the certificate needs only $S_I\succ0$, all algebraic results extend to $Y\in(0,1)$
(Section~\ref{ssec:e0-initial}); only the continuum coercivity in $H^{Y/2}_{M,G}$ keeps the

\subsection{Energy norm and Crank--Nicolson bound}
\label{ssec:riesz}\label{ssec:cn-certificate}
For a fixed query, assume $\Mh\succ0$ and
$\Sh=(\Ah+\Ah^\top)/2\succ0$ on the free space. Define
\begin{equation}
 \|v\|_{\Sh}^2=v^\top\Sh v,\qquad
 \|r\|_{\Sh^{-1}}^2=r^\top\Sh^{-1}r
 =\sup_{v\ne0}\frac{(r^\top v)^2}{v^\top\Sh v}.
 \label{eq:Sh}
\end{equation}
Positivity of $S_I$ holds by Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} with the explicit
constant $\alpha_{\rm lb}(\mu)=\min\sym{s}_{\mu,K}-\varepsilon_K(\mu)>0$.

For an arbitrary candidate trajectory $\{\cc_\theta^n\}_{n=0}^{N_T}$ define the
Crank--Nicolson Galerkin residual
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \rr_\theta^{n+1/2}
 :=\Mh\frac{\cc_\theta^{n+1}-\cc_\theta^n}{\dt}
  +\Ah(\mu)\overline\cc_\theta^{\,n+1/2}
  -\overline\ff^{\,n+1/2},}
 \label{eq:cn-residual}
\end{equation}
and the coefficient error $\ee^n=\cc_h^n-\cc_\theta^n$.  Subtracting
\eqref{eq:cn-residual} from \eqref{eq:cn-reference},
\begin{equation}
 \Mh\frac{\ee^{n+1}-\ee^n}{\dt}+\Ah(\mu)\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2}
 =-\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}.
 \label{eq:discrete-error-equation}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Crank--Nicolson residual certificate]
\label{thm:fully-discrete-certificate}
Let $\Mh$ and $\Sh$ be symmetric positive definite, and let reference and
candidate use identical matrices, forcing and prescribed coefficients.
For every $m=1,\ldots,N_T$,
\begin{equation}
 \|\ee^m\|_{\Mh}^2+
 \dt\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\|\overline\ee^{n+1/2}\|_{\Sh}^2
 \le\|\ee^0\|_{\Mh}^2+
 \dt\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\|\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}\|_{\Sh^{-1}}^2.
 \label{eq:fully-discrete-certificate}
\end{equation}
Consequently, with
\begin{equation}
 \etaSharp^2=\dt\sum_{n=0}^{N_T-1}
 (\rr_\theta^{n+1/2})^\top\Sh^{-1}\rr_\theta^{n+1/2},
 \qquad \eta_{\rm aug,sh}=(\beta_0^2+\etaSharp^2)^{1/2},
 \label{eq:sharp-estimator}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_0\ge\|\ee^0\|_{\Mh}$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\le m\le N_T}\|V_h^m-V_{\theta,h}^m\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
 \le\eta_{\rm aug,sh}.
 \label{eq:sharp-L2}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiply \eqref{eq:discrete-error-equation} on the left by
$(\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2})^\top$.  Because $\Mh$ is symmetric,
\[
 (\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2})^\top\Mh\frac{\ee^{n+1}-\ee^n}{\dt}
 =\frac{1}{2\dt}\bigl(\norm{\ee^{n+1}}_{\Mh}^2-\norm{\ee^n}_{\Mh}^2\bigr),
\]
and, by the definition \eqref{eq:Sh},
\[
 (\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2})^\top\Ah\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2}
 =\norm{\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2}}_{\Sh}^2 .
\]
The residual term obeys
\[
 |(\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2})^\top\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}|
 \le\tfrac12\norm{\overline\ee^{\,n+1/2}}_{\Sh}^2
 +\tfrac12\norm{\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}}_{\Sh^{-1}}^2 .
\]
Multiplying by $2\dt$ and summing over $n=0,\dots,m-1$ telescopes the mass
terms and gives \eqref{eq:fully-discrete-certificate}.  The right-hand side increases with $m$; taking its final value and using
shared-lifting cancellation proves \eqref{eq:sharp-L2}.
\end{proof}

With matching initial vectors, the residual estimator vanishes precisely
when the trajectories coincide: zero residual gives the same uniquely
solvable CN recurrence, since the symmetric part of
$B_+=\Mh+\dt\Ah/2$ is positive definite.
If residual evaluation uses different matrices or forcing, the required
residual is
\[
 \rho_{\rm ref}=\rho_{\rm ev}
 +(A_h-A_{\rm ev})\overline c_\theta+(f_{\rm ev}-f_{\rm ref}).
\]
These discrepancies require allowances in the same dual norm. A computed
reference also carries a solver error allowance
(Section~\ref{ssec:benchmark}).

\subsection{Upper bounds from incomplete Riesz solves}
\label{ssec:guaranteed}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate} contains the exact quantity
$\rr^\top\Sh^{-1}\rr$.  In practice the Riesz problem is solved iteratively,
so the online method needs an upper bound that remains valid before the
iteration has converged.  The bracket below needs a lower bound
\begin{equation}
  0<\alpha_{\rm lb}(\mu)\le \lambda_{\min}(\Sh(\mu)),
  \qquad \alpha_{\rm lb}(\mu):=\min_\vartheta\sym{s}_{\mu,K}(\vartheta)-\varepsilon_K(\mu),
  \label{eq:alpha-lb}
\end{equation}
which Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral} supplies in closed form; no eigenvalue computation enters.
\begin{lemma}[Computable two-sided bracket for the dual norm]
\label{lem:guaranteed}
Let $\Sh\succ0$, let $\rr\in\R^{N_h}$, and let
$0<\alpha_{\rm lb}\le\lambda_{\min}(\Sh)$.  For an arbitrary
$\vv\in\R^{N_h}$ set $\sres:=\rr-\Sh\vv$ and define
\begin{equation}
  \mathfrak l(\vv):=2\vv^\top\rr-\vv^\top\Sh\vv,
  \qquad
  \mathfrak u(\vv):=\mathfrak l(\vv)
    +\frac{\norm{\sres}_2^2}{\alpha_{\rm lb}}.
  \label{eq:riesz-bracket-def}
\end{equation}
Then, for every $\vv$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
   \mathfrak l(\vv)
   \le \rr^\top\Sh^{-1}\rr
   \le \mathfrak u(\vv).}
 \label{eq:riesz-bracket}
\end{equation}
The bracket width is
\begin{equation}
  \mathfrak u(\vv)-\mathfrak l(\vv)
  =\frac{\norm{\sres}_2^2}{\alpha_{\rm lb}},
  \label{eq:bracket-width}
\end{equation}
so it closes as the Riesz residual tends to zero. The upper bracket need
not decrease monotonically with iteration count, because the Euclidean
residual norm need not do so.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $\zz=\Sh^{-1}\rr$,
\[
 \rr^\top\Sh^{-1}\rr-\mathfrak l(\vv)
 = (\vv-\zz)^\top\Sh(\vv-\zz)
 = \sres^\top\Sh^{-1}\sres\ge0.
\]
Since $\Sh\succeq\alpha_{\rm lb}I$,
$\sres^\top\Sh^{-1}\sres\le
\alpha_{\rm lb}^{-1}\norm{\sres}_2^2$, which gives the upper bound and
\eqref{eq:bracket-width}.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Augmented upper certificate]
\label{cor:guaranteed}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate} and
$0<\alpha_{\rm lb}\le\lambda_{\min}(\Sh)$, let $v^{n+1/2}$ be any
approximate Riesz vectors. Define
\begin{equation}
 \etaGuar^2=\dt\sum_{n=0}^{N_T-1}\mathfrak u(v^{n+1/2}),
 \qquad \eta_{\rm aug}=(\beta_0^2+\etaGuar^2)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:guaranteed-estimator}
\end{equation}
Then $\max_m\|\ee^m\|_{\Mh}\le\eta_{\rm aug}$, independently of the
iterative stopping error. As the Riesz residuals vanish,
$\etaGuar\to\etaSharp$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:guaranteed} at each time step and use
Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate}.
\end{proof}
PCG effort controls bracket sharpness. The zero iterate gives the upper
bound $\|\rr\|_2^2/\alpha_{\rm lb}$; convergence gives the exact Riesz value.

\begin{remark}[Arithmetic scope]
\label{rem:arithmetic-scope}
The theorems and brackets are exact-arithmetic statements for the assembled matrices.
The only quantities not enclosed are the floating-point residual evaluation and the
accumulation: if a computed residual $\widehat\rho_n$ differs from the required residual by at
most $d_n$ in the $S_h^{-1}$ norm and $u_n$ is an upper bound for
$\widehat\rho_n^\top S_h^{-1}\widehat\rho_n$, then
$\widehat\eta^2=\beta_0^2+\sum_n\dt\,(\sqrt{u_n}+d_n)^2$ is valid.  The same allowance
structure absorbs the affine-operator error of Theorem~\ref{thm:eim-cert}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Efficiency and spot-price bounds}
\label{ssec:efficiency}\label{ssec:greeks-cert}
For $\Sh\succ0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_h=\|\Sh^{-1/2}\Ah\Sh^{-1/2}\|_2\ge1,
 \qquad
 |||\ee|||^2=\dt\sum_n
 \left(\|\Mh\partial\ee^{n+1/2}\|_{\Sh^{-1}}^2
       +\|\overline\ee^{n+1/2}\|_{\Sh}^2\right),
 \label{eq:kappa-def}
\end{equation}
where $\partial\ee^{n+1/2}=(\ee^{n+1}-\ee^n)/\dt$.
\begin{theorem}[Matrix-dependent efficiency]
\label{thm:efficiency}
For an assembled query with $\Sh\succ0$,
\begin{equation}
 \etaSharp^2\le2\max\{1,\kappa_h^2\}\,|||\ee|||^2.
 \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}
Under exact assembly, $\kappa_h\le C_{\rm cont}/c_1$, uniformly in the
mesh and parameter family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The error equation gives
$\|\rr\|_{\Sh^{-1}}\le\|\Mh\partial\ee\|_{\Sh^{-1}}
+\kappa_h\|\overline\ee\|_{\Sh}$. Square, use
$(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2$, and sum. The exact-assembly estimate follows from
continuity and coercivity; see Appendix~\ref{ssec:exact-assembly}.
\end{proof}
This efficiency norm contains a time-derivative term; it differs from the
maximum-in-time mass error in the reliability estimate.
For zero initial error, the error equation also yields
$|||\ee|||^2\le(3+2\kappa_h^2)\etaSharp^2$.
Effectivity is measured as the augmented bound divided by
$\max_m\|\ee^m\|_{\Mh}$.

For $\Phi_I(x)$ the free-basis evaluation vector, define
\begin{equation}
 \beta_I(x)=\sqrt{\Phi_I(x)^\top M_I^{-1}\Phi_I(x)}.
 \label{eq:gamma-infty}
\end{equation}
Cauchy--Schwarz in the mass pairing gives the discrete spot-price bound
\begin{equation}
 |V_h^m(x)-V_{\theta,h}^m(x)|\le\beta_I(x)\eta_{\rm aug}.
 \label{eq:greek-Linf}
\end{equation}
It converts a trajectory tolerance into a price tolerance at specified
spots. Derivative bounds and continuum error separation are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{ssec:derivatives}.

\begin{proposition}[Guaranteed acceptance]
\label{prop:two-stage}
Assume the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate},
exact residual evaluation, and $\beta_0\ge\|\ee^0\|_{\Mh}$. Let $\eta_{\rm aug}$ be
$\eta^{\bar X}_{\rm aug}$, $\eta^X_{\rm aug}$ or $(\beta_0^2+\etaGuar^2)^{1/2}$ with $\alpha_{\rm lb}$ from \eqref{eq:alpha-lb}.
If the rule in Figure~\ref{alg:pcc} returns
\textsc{reduced accept}, \textsc{banded accept}, \textsc{exact accept} or \textsc{corrected accept} with $\eta_{\rm aug}\le\varepsilon$, then
\begin{equation}
  \max_{1\le m\le N_T}\norm{V_h^m-V_{\theta,h}^m}_{L^2(\Omega)}
  \le \eta_{\rm aug}\le\varepsilon .
  \label{eq:soundness}
\end{equation}
The conclusion is independent of the PCG stopping error, by Lemma~\ref{lem:guaranteed} for
$\etaGuar$, by Corollary~\ref{cor:cheap} for $\eta^X$ and by Proposition~\ref{prop:threestage} for $\eta^{\bar X}$. The same proof
applies to a corrected trajectory whose recomputed augmented bound passes
(\textsc{corrected accept}) and to a direct fallback (\textsc{qualified fallback}).
When $\ee^0=0$, one may take $\beta_0=0$ and recover the residual-only
rule. Bounded evaluation discrepancies additionally require the allowances
of Remark~\ref{rem:arithmetic-scope}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At every time step Lemma~\ref{lem:guaranteed} bounds
$\rr^\top\Sh^{-1}\rr$ from above by the computable upper bracket used in
$\etaGuar$.  Summing these bounds and applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate}, with the initial term bounded
by $\beta_0^2$, proves \eqref{eq:soundness}.  
\end{proof}

Let $\dd^n$ be a correction trajectory, $\cc^n_{\rm corr}=\cc_\theta^n+\dd^n$.
The exact correction recovering the reference trajectory solves
\begin{equation}
  \Mh\frac{\dd^{n+1}-\dd^n}{\dt}+\Ah\overline\dd^{\,n+1/2}
  =-\rr_\theta^{n+1/2},
  \qquad \dd^0=\cc_h^0-\cc_\theta^0=\ee^0.
  \label{eq:correction-equation}
\end{equation}
The initial correction is zero only when the candidate reproduces the
discrete initial target. In free coordinates it is
$\dd_I^0=\zz_h^0-\zz_\theta^0$; the prescribed coefficients already
match after boundary projection. With $B_\pm=\Mh\pm\tfrac{\dt}{2}\Ah$,
\begin{equation}
  B_+\dd^{n+1}=B_-\dd^n-\dt\,\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}.
  \label{eq:correction-step}
\end{equation}
Solving \eqref{eq:correction-equation} exactly is equivalent to recomputing
the reference solution, so the practical aim is to apply a small number of
Krylov iterations warm-started with the candidate coefficients at each
step, while retaining the specified initial correction.


Uniqueness of the CN recurrence implies that an exact correction recovers
$\cc_h$. An approximate correction is accepted only after recomputing its
augmented bound. A conditional Richardson estimate is given in
Appendix~\ref{ssec:correction-extension}; the numerical implementation
uses GMRES.

a projection-based reduced model, a neural reduced model and a neural operator, and the
structure that makes the first two certifiable at a cost independent of $N$: the operator
family $\{\Ah(\mu)\}$ is, to a stated accuracy, affine in a small number of
parameter-independent Toeplitz matrices.
\subsection{Affine decomposition of the fractional blocks}
\label{ssec:eim}
The operator depends on $\mu$ through the scalars $\rf-\omega_S$, $\lambda_0$, $C\Gamma(-Y)h^{-Y}$
and, non-affinely, through $(G,M,Y)$ in the fractional symbol.  The reduced-basis literature
handles such dependence by the empirical interpolation method
\cite{BarraultMadayNguyenPatera2004,GreplMadayNguyenPatera2007}; what is specific here is that
the interpolation acts on the symbol, a function of one variable, rather than on the matrices,
and that the interpolation error transfers to the operator norm through
Lemma~\ref{lem:toeplitz} without any constant.
\input{theory/lem_eim}
Greedy empirical interpolation of $\{\Phi_\nu\}$ on a $16\times16\times16$ training grid in
$(\log G,\log M,Y)$ selects magic points $\vartheta_1,\dots,\vartheta_Q$ and parameters
$\nu_1,\dots,\nu_Q$ such that $\ell(\nu)$ is the solution of a $Q\times Q$ triangular system; the
family is analytic in $(\delta,Y)$ with its nearest singularity at $\delta=0$, so the
interpolation error decays geometrically in $Q$ at a rate set by $G_{\max}/G_{\min}$ and
$M_{\max}/M_{\min}$ rather than by $h$.  Figure~\ref{fig:eim} shows the test error on $1000$ Sobol parameters and the singular values
of the training snapshots: $Q=23$, $26$, $27$, $29$, $29$ terms reach a relative sup-norm error of
$10^{-9}$ at $N=64$, $128$, $256$, $512$, $1024$, and the curves for $N=512$ and $1024$ coincide,
so the rank saturates in $h$.  For the operator the relevant number is $\varepsilon_Q$ relative to
$\norm{\Ah}_2$: at most $1.7\times10^{-9}$ ($N=64$) and $5.7\times10^{-10}$ ($N=512$) with these $Q$,
with the observed operator-norm error never exceeding $0.93\,\varepsilon_Q$.  The operational rank
is chosen per target tolerance by the condition $\varepsilon_Q\norm{\bar\cc}_2\sqrt{T/\alpha_{\rm lb}}
\le\varepsilon/10$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:eim-cert}: $Q=36$ at $\varepsilon_N$ and $Q=37$ at
$\varepsilon_N/10$ for the production configuration of Section~\ref{ssec:calibration}.

The error $\varepsilon_Q(\mu)$ is evaluated, not estimated: it is the sup-norm of the
interpolation error of the exact symbol at the query with the grid-to-continuum margin of
Lemma~\ref{lem:eim}.  Evaluated on the assembly grid it costs $O(LK)$ operations, which grows with
$N$; the implementation instead evaluates it on a fixed set of $161$ nodes (the graded grid of
Section~\ref{ssec:spectral}, coarsened) with the cell-wise derivative margin, which bounds the
continuous sup-norm and hence, by Remark~\ref{rem:compression}, the assembled operator difference,
at a cost of about $1.6$~ms independent of $N$ and at most $2.1$ times the value on the full grid.

(Section~\ref{ssec:ood}).

\subsection{Candidates: coefficient-valued neural map}
\label{ssec:target}\label{ssec:architectures}\label{ssec:exact-ic}
A candidate predicts coefficients in the fixed spline space:
\begin{equation}
 (\mu,T,\psi,\tau)\longmapsto\cc_\theta(\tau;\mu,T,\psi),
 \qquad U_{\theta,h}=\sum_k c_{\theta,k}\phi_{h,k}.
 \label{eq:operator-map}
\end{equation}
Prescribed coefficients are overwritten at every time level before
certification. This projection makes the returned candidate admissible in
the same affine space as the reference. Residuals use the assembled operator
of Section~\ref{ssec:assembly}, and spline derivatives are available analytically.
The neural-operator candidate is a DeepONet with payoff and parameter branches and a time
trunk, latent dimension 64 and 80,002 trainable parameters, followed by a coefficient decoder
that supplies the spline-valued output. The experiments fix $\mathcal K=100$;
functional-input notation accommodates a payoff encoder but the benchmark
tests a parameter map for this single strike.  It was trained on the principal-period discretisation of the earlier version of this work, and
we did not retrain it on the exact-assembly references.  Certified as a candidate of the exact
system ($N=64$, $N_T=60$, 120 benchmark queries), it is rejected at every tolerance of the sweep
$10^{-3}\times\{0.1,\dots,10\}$: the median true error is $0.78$ (relative $3.1\times10^{-3}$), the
median exact bound $3.4$, with no false accept.  The certificate thus measures, correctly, the
distance between two discrete problems; the neural candidate of the production study is the
POD-NN below.

The initial vector is the solver's specified target: solve the raw mass
projection $\bar M\bar\cc^0=\bar\bb^0$, with
$\bar b_k^0=(\psi,\phi_{h,k})_{L^2(\Omega)}$, then impose the four prescribed
coefficients. Its free part is $\zz_h^0=E^\top\cc_h^0$.
This projection followed by overwriting generally differs from the
constrained mass projection; all reference and training trajectories use
the former. The neural model embeds this target as
\begin{equation}
 \cc_\theta(\tau)=\cc_h^0+\tau\mathcal N_\theta(\tau,\mu,T,\psi).
 \label{eq:exact-ic}
\end{equation}
The initial discrepancy
$\beta_0=\|\zz_h^0-\zz_\theta^0\|_{M_I}$ is evaluated explicitly for every
candidate. It vanishes for the neural checkpoints and is generally nonzero
for reduced trajectories.

\subsection{Training objective and data}
\label{ssec:losses}\label{sec:data}
The relative mass-weighted data loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm data}
 =\frac{(\cc_\theta-\cc_h)^\top\Mh(\cc_\theta-\cc_h)}
        {\cc_h^\top\Mh\cc_h+\epsilon},
 \label{eq:data-loss}
\end{equation}
which is the relative squared $L^2(\Omega)$ error of the homogeneous
spline field, with a small regularisation $\epsilon>0$ in the denominator.

A compatible residual regulariser is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm res}
 =\frac{1}{N_T}\sum_{n=0}^{N_T-1}
 (\rr_\theta^{n+1/2})^\top\Sh(\mu)^{-1}\rr_\theta^{n+1/2},
 \label{eq:residual-loss}
\end{equation}
with $\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}$ given by \eqref{eq:cn-residual}, and the hybrid
objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\rm data}+\lambda_R\mathcal L_{\rm res}.
 \label{eq:hybrid-loss}
\end{equation}

For a uniform grid the time-accumulated residual satisfies
$\etaSharp^2=T\mathcal L_{\rm res}$. It controls absolute mass error together
with the initial term; it is distinct from the normalised data loss.

The training set consists of 800 Sobol-sampled trajectories (seed 0) of the exact-assembly
reference at the production resolution, with a separate 30-query validation set (seed 999999)
used for checkpoint selection, and a 4000-trajectory set for the data-size study.
Table~\ref{tab:training} lists the optimiser, schedule, parameter counts and wall times.
Evaluation uses fixed checkpoints,
with compatibility checks for the mesh, time grid and parameter box; the training scripts are
part of the archive (Section~\ref{ssec:archive}).

\subsection{Mass-weighted POD trajectories}
\label{ssec:pod-construction}
With $M_I=LL^\top$ and the uncentred free snapshot matrix $Z$, define
$V=L^{-\top}U_r$, where $U_r$ contains the leading left singular vectors of
$L^\top Z$. Then $V^\top M_IV=I$ and the subspace minimises the aggregate
training-snapshot projection error in the mass norm. The 800 trajectories
supply 192,800 snapshots ($241$ time levels each). The reduced equations are
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\alpha^{n+1}-\alpha^n}{\dt}
 + (V^\top A_I V)\frac{\alpha^{n+1}+\alpha^n}{2}
 = V^\top f_I^{n+1/2},\qquad
 \alpha^0=V^\top M_I \zz_h^0.
 \label{eq:repaired-pod-cn}
\end{equation}

The reconstructed free candidate $V\alpha^n$ shares the prescribed boundary
coefficients. Its finite-rank initial discrepancy $\beta_0=\norm{(I-VV^\top M_I)\zz_h^0}_{M_I}$
is the projection error of the initial target and enters the same certificate as the neural
candidate.

\paragraph{POD--Galerkin with the affine operator.}
With $A_Q$ in place of $A_I$ the reduced operator is
\[
V^\top A_Q(\mu)V=-(\rf-\omega_S)\,V^\top D_IV+\lambda_0 I-C\Gamma(-Y)h^{-Y}\sum_q\ell_q(\nu)\,V^\top T_qV,
\]
a combination of $Q+2$ precomputed $r\times r$ matrices with scalar coefficients, and the
reduced forcing is affine in the same scalars and in the two lifting values $b_L(\tau_n)$, through
the precomputed vectors $V^\top M_I e_{\rm bd}$, $V^\top T_q e_{\rm bd}$.  The online solve therefore
costs $O(Qr^2+N_Tr^2)$ operations and never touches the full space.  The candidate is certified
either at full order, by Theorem~\ref{thm:eim-cert} with $\eta^X$ or $\eta^S$ and the allowance
term, or online as follows.

\paragraph{Reduced online certificate.}
Fix a comparison matrix $\bar X=r_{\min}M_I+\bar\beta\,h^2R^{(1)}_I$ per cell of a coarse
partition of the $(C,Y,\min(G,M))$ range, with $\bar\beta=0.99\min_{\rm cell}\beta_\mu$ evaluated on
a grid of the cell (Remark~\ref{rem:reduced-online}).  Then $\rho^\top\bar X^{-1}\rho$ is a quadratic form in the
$Q+2$ scalar coefficients and the reduced states, whose $(Q+2)^2$ Gram blocks
$R_q^\top\bar X^{-1}R_{q'}$, $R_q\in\{M_IV,\,T_qV,\,D_IV,\,\text{forcing directions}\}$, are
computed offline by banded solves.  Online, $\eta^{\bar X}$ costs $O(\varrho\,(Q+2)(2r+1))$ operations
with $\varrho$ retained rows (below), independently of $N_h$, and is an upper bound for the exact dual norm without any further factor
(Remark~\ref{rem:reduced-online}); the symbol quantities that accompany it, $\varepsilon_Q(\mu)$ and
the optional tightening factor, are evaluated on an $N$-independent graded grid with derivative
margins.  Two implementation facts matter.  First, the Gram-tensor form of the offline/online
decomposition is numerically unusable here: for accurate candidates the residual is $10^{-8}$ of
the terms it is assembled from, and the Gram form loses all digits.  The implementation uses the
$R$-factor form \cite{CasenaveErnLelievre2014,BuhrEngwerOhlbergerRave2014}, compressed offline by a
singular value decomposition with a rigorous term for the discarded part; the online cost is then
$O(\varrho\,(Q+2)(2r+1))$ with $\varrho$ retained rows ($185$--$1022$ for $r=8$--$128$ at $N=1024$;
$\varrho$ saturates below $N_h$, which is what makes the cost independent of $N$).  The online
value agrees with the full-order $\rho^\top\bar X^{-1}\rho$ to $3\times10^{-15}$ for $O(1)$ residuals
and to $2\times10^{-10}$ for POD--Galerkin candidates, the latter being the double-precision floor
(confirmed in extended precision); the compressed bound exceeds the uncompressed one by at most
$5\times10^{-4}$ relative.  Second, the cell norm costs a factor $1.87$ (median) against the
query's own $X_I$ and about $4$ against $\eta^S$ (Table~\ref{tab:pod-ranks}).  On an idle core the
complete online certificate (interpolation coefficients, $\varepsilon_Q$, quadratic form) takes
$2.9$, $3.9$, $4.6$, $5.6$, $6.8$, $8.3$~ms at $r=8$, $48$, $64$, $80$, $96$, $128$ ($Q=36$), of which
$2.7$~ms are rank-independent; the reduced solve takes $0.7$--$2.0$~ms.  The offline tensors
occupy $9$~MB ($r=8$) to $688$~MB ($r=128$) for the 18 cells.

\paragraph{POD-NN.}
The neural reduced candidate maps $(\mu,T)$ to the $r\times N_T$ matrix of POD coefficients
$\alpha^1,\dots,\alpha^{N_T}$ by a multilayer perceptron, with $\alpha^0=V^\top M_I\zz_h^0$ fixed
so that $\beta_0$ is again the projection error only
\cite{HesthavenUbbiali2018}.  Its training objective is the least-squares loss in the reduced
coordinates (the mass-weighted loss \eqref{eq:data-loss}, since $V^\top M_IV=I$) plus
$\lambda_R$ times the reduced online certificate $\eta^{\bar X}_{\rm aug}$ of the previous paragraph
(not squared), which is a differentiable function of the network output and costs nothing per
sample; it plays the role of the residual regulariser \eqref{eq:residual-loss}, with the exact dual
norm replaced by a rigorous majorant.  The network is evaluated in double precision: single-precision rounding of the
predicted coefficients alone produces residuals whose certified bound is of the order $10^{-3}$ at
$N=512$, i.e.\ the tolerance itself.  The standard output has $r\times N_T=11\,520$ coefficients ($3.09$~M parameters, of which $2.96$~M in
the output layer).  A second variant predicts the coefficients of a temporal POD of the reduced
trajectories, $r\times r_t$ with $r_t=23$ modes chosen so that the temporal projection error is
below $\varepsilon_N/10$ ($0.42$~M parameters); it removes the time-difference noise that the
residual penalises by construction.  Both are trained with $\lambda_R\in\{0,10^{-2},10^{-1},1\}$ on
800 and 4000 trajectories (Table~\ref{tab:training}).

\subsection{Cost model}
\label{ssec:cost}
Let ${\rm it}_B$ be the mean number of preconditioned GMRES iterations per Crank--Nicolson step
(Table~\ref{tab:pcg-v9}: $5$--$9$), and count one FFT of length $2N_h$ as $5N_h\log_2N_h$
flops.  Per query,
\begin{align*}
 \text{direct solve:}&\quad N_T\,{\rm it}_B\,(2\cdot5N_h\log_2N_h+O(N_h)),\\
 \text{full-order certificate }(\eta^X):&\quad N_T\,(2\cdot5N_h\log_2N_h)+N_T(2p-1)N_h+O(p^2N_h),\\
 \text{full-order certificate }(\eta^S):&\quad N_T\,{\rm it}_{\rm PCG}\,(2\cdot5N_h\log_2N_h)+\text{the above},\\
 \text{reduced solve:}&\quad (Q+2)r^2+N_T\,r^2,\\
 \text{reduced certificate }(\eta^{\bar X}):&\quad \varrho\,(Q+2)(2r+1)+N_T\,r\,(Q+2),\qquad \varrho\le N_h\ \text{retained rows},
\end{align*}
plus the symbol evaluation $O(LK)$ common to all certified variants.  Two conclusions are
independent of the implementation.  First, at full order the certificate $\eta^X$ costs one
residual evaluation per step, i.e.\ about $1/{\rm it}_B$ of the direct solve: in one space
dimension with FFT-applied Toeplitz operators the certificate can be at most $5$--$10$ times
cheaper than the solve it certifies, whatever the candidate.  Second, the reduced solve and the
reduced certificate do not involve $N_h$ once the offline compression has fixed $\varrho$; the
scalable statement of this paper is that a certified reduced query costs $O(\varrho\,Q\,r)$
operations at any resolution, with the rigorous link to the full-order problem supplied by
Theorem~\ref{thm:eim-cert} and Lemma~\ref{lem:toeplitz}.
Table~\ref{tab:cost} in Section~\ref{ssec:benchmark} gives the measured constants.

\section{Interpretation and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}
The discrete problem of this paper is the exact spline Galerkin system of the CGMY operator, and
the paper's first point is that for shift-invariant bases ``exact'' is cheap: the matrices are
Toeplitz sections of a periodised symbol whose alias series is summed to a stated tail, and the
same symbol gives, in closed form, the spectral lower bound, the banded comparison norm and the
affine structure on which everything else rests.  Positivity of the symmetric part, which the
earlier version of this work assessed by an audit of 459 parameter points and a numerical
eigenvalue estimate, is a theorem with the explicit constant $\alpha_{\rm lb}=\rf-\varepsilon_K$,
and it holds for $Y\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}$.

The second point is about the cost of a guarantee.  At full order the certificate is one residual
evaluation per time step plus a banded solve, about $5\%$ of the direct solve at every resolution
tested; in one space dimension with FFT-applied operators it cannot be much less, because the
solve itself is a handful of residual evaluations per step.  The scalable statement is the reduced
one: with the affine operator and a fixed comparison norm per cell of parameter space, the
certificate of a reduced trajectory costs a few milliseconds independently of $N$, and the
three-stage rule escalates to the full-order bounds only for candidates within a known factor of
the tolerance.  On the benchmark this gives a certified one-cent price in $0.03$ of the direct time
from rank $80$ on, and on $10^4$ queries $99.8\%$ of the verdicts are settled without touching the
full space.

The third point is the negative one.  No neural reduced model trained here comes within a factor
$46$ of the tolerance, although the basis it predicts in certifies at $3\times10^{-3}$ and the
projection-based reduced model on the same basis passes at rank $56$--$64$.  The certificate is
what establishes this, query by query, at no cost and with no false accept on any of the $150$
in-box and $264$ out-of-box queries with a reference solution, and it also explains it: the residual penalises temporal roughness that
a least-squares fit of the coefficients does not see, so the networks that the rigorous bound
selects are the smoother, less accurate ones, and the space--time representation that removes the
roughness by construction is the best of them.  Whether a better architecture, more data or a
different loss closes the remaining gap is a question the framework can now answer at the price of
a few milliseconds per candidate.

Outside the training box the same certificate accepts $92\%$ ($193$ of $210$) of the queries on which the reduced
model still delivers one cent, where any detector based on the box itself would discard them all;
this is the operational meaning of a bound that depends on nothing but the discrete equations.
What the certificate does not do is bound the continuum price: the resolution was chosen so that
the discretisation error is below one cent on the diagnostic queries, including two corners that
required $N=1024$, and the certified bound is added to that budget, not instead of it.  Two further
limitations are stated where they arise: the grid evaluation of the symbol extrema is not a
validated enclosure (Section~\ref{ssec:spectral}), and the floating-point evaluation of the
residual is covered by an allowance rather than by interval arithmetic
(Remark~\ref{rem:arithmetic-scope}).

The American put is the natural next target.  Theorem~\ref{thm:vi} in Appendix~\ref{ssec:vi} gives
the residual certificate for the backward-Euler discrete variational inequality in the same spline
space, with the complementarity term as the only addition; exact assembly, the spectral bound, the
banded norm and the affine operator apply verbatim, and a certified reduced model for the
American price is a matter of the obstacle solver and training data, not of new theory.

\section{Uniform stability and supporting derivations}
\label{app:proofs}
\subsection{Explicit constants and proof of uniform stability}
\label{app:proof-uniform}\label{ssec:lk-coercivity}
For the box \eqref{eq:parameter-set}, define
\begin{equation}
  a_\ast:=\max\{M_{\max},G_{\max}\},\qquad
  \kappa_Y:=\frac{2e^{-\pi}}{(2-Y)\pi^{Y}},\qquad
  \Theta_w:=\max\{1,M_{\max}^2,G_{\max}^2\}^{Y_{\max}/2}.
  \label{eq:uniform-constants-aux}
\end{equation}
Explicit admissible choices are
\begin{equation}
  c_1
  =\min_{\mu\in\Pset}\min\Bigl\{
    \frac{\rf}{1+2^{1+Y/2}a_\ast^{Y}},\;
    \frac{C\,\kappa_Y}{2^{Y/2}}
  \Bigr\}
  =\frac{r_{\min}}{1+2^{1+Y_{\max}/2}a_\ast^{Y_{\max}}},
  \label{eq:c1-explicit}
\end{equation}
the second branch being larger on the box of Table~\ref{tab:proposed-box}, and
\begin{equation}
  C_{\rm cont}
  =2\Bigl(
    \Omega_\ast
    +2C_{\max}\gamma_{\max}\max\{1,a_\ast^2\}^{Y_{\max}/2}
    +\lambda_\ast
  \Bigr),
  \label{eq:Ccont-explicit}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\gamma_{\max}:=\!\!\max_{Y\in[Y_{\min},Y_{\max}]}\!\!\Gamma(-Y)<\infty,
\qquad
\Omega_\ast:=\sup_{\mu\in\Pset}|\rf-\omega_S(\mu)|,
\qquad
\lambda_\ast:=\sup_{\mu\in\Pset}\lambda_0(\mu),
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:uniform-aux-constants}
\end{equation}
the last two being finite.
Throughout, $\mu=(C,G,M,Y,\rf)\in\Pset$ and $s_\mu=Y/2\in(1/2,1)$.  We work on
the full line and transfer to $\Omega$ at the end using
the zero-extension convention
\eqref{eq:energy-space}, which preserves all constants.
\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Step 1: uniform equivalence of the parameter-dependent norm.}
Let $s\in[Y_{\min}/2,Y_{\max}/2]\subset(1/2,1)$ and let
$w_\mu(\xi):=1+|M-i\xi|^{2s}+|G+i\xi|^{2s}
=1+(M^2+\xi^2)^{s}+(G^2+\xi^2)^{s}$ be the weight in
\eqref{eq:HMG-norm}.  Since $s\le1$ and $(a+b)^s\le a^s+b^s$ for $s\in(0,1]$,
\[
  w_\mu(\xi)\ \le\ 1+2\max\{1,M_{\max}^2,G_{\max}^2\}^{s}(1+\xi^2)^{s}
  \ \le\ (1+2\Theta_w)(1+\xi^2)^{s},
\]
with $\Theta_w=\max\{1,M_{\max}^2,G_{\max}^2\}^{Y_{\max}/2}$, using
$(1+\xi^2)^s\ge1$.  For the lower bound, $w_\mu(\xi)\ge1+|\xi|^{2s}$ and,
since $(1+\xi^2)^s\le2^s\max\{1,|\xi|^{2s}\}\le2(1+|\xi|^{2s})$ for
$s\le1$,
\[
  w_\mu(\xi)\ \ge\ \tfrac12(1+\xi^2)^{s}.
\]
Hence, uniformly in $\mu\in\Pset$,
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac12\norm{u}_{H^{s_\mu}(\R)}^2
  \ \le\ \norm{u}_{H^{s_\mu}_{M,G}(\R)}^2
  \ \le\ (1+2\Theta_w)\norm{u}_{H^{s_\mu}(\R)}^2 ,
  \label{eq:uniform-norm-equivalence}
\end{equation}
where $\norm{u}_{H^{s}(\R)}^2=\tfrac1{2\pi}\int(1+\xi^2)^{s}|\widehat u|^2$.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Step 2: uniform continuity.}
From \eqref{eq:symbol}, for every $\xi$,
\[
  |A_\mu(\xi)|
  \le|\rf-\omega_S||\xi|
   +C\Gamma(-Y)\bigl[(M^2+\xi^2)^{Y/2}+(G^2+\xi^2)^{Y/2}\bigr]
   +\lambda_0 .
\]
The map $(C,G,M,Y)\mapsto\omega_S$ of \eqref{eq:martingale-correction} is
continuous on $\Pset$ because $(M-1)^Y$ is continuous and finite on the selected box
(and remains so at $M=1$), and $\Gamma(-Y)$ is continuous on $[Y_{\min},Y_{\max}]\subset(1,2)$;
hence $\Omega_\ast<\infty$ and $\lambda_\ast<\infty$.  Since $Y>1$ we have
$|\xi|\le(1+\xi^2)^{Y/2}$, and
$(a^2+\xi^2)^{Y/2}\le\max\{1,a^2\}^{Y/2}(1+\xi^2)^{Y/2}$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
  |A_\mu(\xi)|
  \le\Bigl(\Omega_\ast+2C_{\max}\gamma_{\max}\max\{1,a_\ast^2\}^{Y_{\max}/2}
   +\lambda_\ast\Bigr)(1+\xi^2)^{s_\mu}.
  \label{eq:uniform-symbol-upper}
\end{equation}
By Plancherel's theorem and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
  |a_\R(u,w)|
  \le\Bigl(\sup_{\xi\in\R}\frac{|A_\mu(\xi)|}{(1+\xi^2)^{s_\mu}}\Bigr)
  \norm{u}_{H^{s_\mu}(\R)}\norm{w}_{H^{s_\mu}(\R)},
\]
and
\eqref{eq:uniform-norm-equivalence} converts the $H^{s_\mu}$ norms into
$H^{s_\mu}_{M,G}$ norms at the cost of a factor $2$.  This gives
\eqref{eq:Ccont-explicit}.


\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Step 3: the L\'evy--Khintchine form.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:LK},
\begin{equation}
  \Real A_\mu(\xi)=\rf+C\,[I_G(\xi)+I_M(\xi)],\qquad
  I_a(\xi)=\int_0^\infty(1-\cos\xi x)\,e^{-ax}x^{-1-Y}dx\ \ge0,
  \label{eq:uniform-low-freq}
\end{equation}
so $\Real A_\mu(\xi)\ge \rf\ge r_{\min}>0$ for all $\xi$; the drift term is purely
imaginary and does not enter.  (This is where $r_{\min}>0$ is used.)  Two elementary
lower bounds for $I_a$ follow from $1-\cos u\ge 2u^2/\pi^2$ on $|u|\le\pi$: restricting the
integral to $0<x\le\pi/|\xi|$ and substituting $u=ax$,
\begin{equation}
  I_a(\xi)\ \ge\ \frac{2\xi^2}{\pi^2}\,a^{Y-2}\!\int_0^{a\pi/|\xi|}\!u^{1-Y}e^{-u}\,du
  \ \ge\ \frac{2}{\pi^2}\,\gamma_Y\,a^{Y-2}\xi^2\quad(|\xi|\le a),
  \qquad \gamma_Y:=\int_0^\pi u^{1-Y}e^{-u}\,du,
  \label{eq:LK-low}
\end{equation}
and, bounding $e^{-ax}\ge e^{-\pi}$ on $0<x\le\pi/|\xi|$ when $|\xi|\ge a$,
\begin{equation}
  I_a(\xi)\ \ge\ e^{-\pi}\frac{2\xi^2}{\pi^2}\int_0^{\pi/|\xi|}x^{1-Y}dx
  =\frac{2e^{-\pi}}{(2-Y)\pi^{Y}}\,|\xi|^{Y}=\kappa_Y|\xi|^Y\quad(|\xi|\ge a).
  \label{eq:LK-high}
\end{equation}
Both bounds hold for every $Y\in(0,2)$; the hypothesis $Y>1$ is needed only to
control the drift by the $H^{Y/2}$ norm in Step~2.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Step 4: the weight.}
The weight of \eqref{eq:HMG-norm} with $q=s_\mu=Y/2$ is
$w_\mu(\xi)=1+(M^2+\xi^2)^{Y/2}+(G^2+\xi^2)^{Y/2}$.  Since
$(a^2+\xi^2)^{Y/2}\le 2^{Y/2}\max\{a,|\xi|\}^{Y}$,
\begin{equation}
  w_\mu(\xi)\le 1+2^{1+Y/2}a_\ast^{Y}\quad(|\xi|\le a_\ast),\qquad
  w_\mu(\xi)\le 1+2^{1+Y/2}|\xi|^{Y}\quad(|\xi|\ge a_\ast).
  \label{eq:weight-bounds}
\end{equation}

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Step 5: combination.}
For $|\xi|\le a_\ast$, \eqref{eq:uniform-low-freq} and \eqref{eq:weight-bounds} give
$\Real A_\mu(\xi)\ge \rf\,(1+2^{1+Y/2}a_\ast^{Y})^{-1}w_\mu(\xi)$.
For $|\xi|\ge a_\ast\ge\max\{G,M\}$, \eqref{eq:LK-high} applies to both $I_G$ and $I_M$, so
$\Real A_\mu(\xi)\ge \rf+2C\kappa_Y|\xi|^Y$, and with \eqref{eq:weight-bounds} and
$(\alpha+\beta)/(\gamma+\delta)\ge\min\{\alpha/\gamma,\beta/\delta\}$,
\[
  \frac{\Real A_\mu(\xi)}{w_\mu(\xi)}\ \ge\ \min\Bigl\{\rf,\ \frac{2C\kappa_Y}{2^{1+Y/2}}\Bigr\}
  \ \ge\ \min\Bigl\{\frac{\rf}{1+2^{1+Y/2}a_\ast^{Y}},\ \frac{C\kappa_Y}{2^{Y/2}}\Bigr\}.
\]
Hence $\Real A_\mu(\xi)\ge c_1\,w_\mu(\xi)$ for all $\xi$ with $c_1$ as in
\eqref{eq:c1-explicit}, and Plancherel's theorem gives
$\Real a_\R(u,u)\ge c_1\norm{u}_{H^{s_\mu}_{M,G}(\R)}^2$ directly in the weighted norm,
without the equivalence factor of Step~1.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Step 6: transfer to $\Omega$.}
For $v,w\in\Vmu$ the zero extensions satisfy
$\widetilde v,\widetilde w\in H^{s_\mu}(\R)$ with
$\norm{\widetilde v}_{L^2(\R)}=\norm{v}_{L^2(\Omega)}$, and by definition
$a_\mu(v,w)=a_\R(\widetilde v,\widetilde w)$ and
$\norm{v}_{\Vmu}=\norm{\widetilde v}_{H^{s_\mu}_{M,G}(\R)}$.  Both estimates
therefore transfer with identical constants, independent of $h$ and of
$\Omega$.  Finally, for real-valued $v$ the form $a_\mu(v,v)$ is real, so
$a_\mu(v,v)=\Real a_\mu(v,v)$.  \qed


The constants are explicit admissible choices rather than optimal ones.
On the box of Table~\ref{tab:proposed-box}, $c_1=5.11\times10^{-5}$ (attained at
$Y=Y_{\max}$, $\rf=r_{\min}$, $a_\ast=M_{\max}=9$) and $C_{\rm cont}\approx2.5\times10^3$.
The infimum of $\Real A_\mu(\xi)/w_\mu(\xi)$ over the box is in fact attained at $\xi=0$, where
\eqref{eq:uniform-low-freq} is an equality: its value $r_{\min}/(1+M_{\max}^{Y_{\max}}+G_{\max}^{Y_{\max}})
=1.40\times10^{-4}$ is confirmed numerically over $544$ box points and $\xi\in[0,10^6]$
(\texttt{results/coercivity\_c1.json}), so the explicit constant is within a factor three of the
optimal one.  The earlier version of this appendix obtained $c_1\approx1.6\times10^{-8}$ from a
high-frequency argument that required $Y_{\min}>1$ at every step; the L\'evy--Khintchine form
removes both the loss and that requirement from the coercivity part of the proof.

\subsection{Exact-assembly consequences and constrained dual norms}
\label{ssec:exact-assembly}
Under exact assembly, $z^\top\Sh z=a_\mu(z_h,z_h)$, so uniform coercivity
implies $\Sh\succ0$. The symmetric parts of $\Mh+\dt\Ah/2$ and
$\Mh+\dt\Ah$ are positive definite, giving nonsingularity.
\begin{lemma}[Positive-rate mass lower bound for exact assembly]
\label{lem:mass-lower}
Under exact assembly (Section~\ref{ssec:exact-assembly}), for
$\mu\in\Pset$ (so $\rf>0$),
\begin{equation}
  \Sh(\mu)\succeq \rf\,\Mh
  \label{eq:S-ge-rM}
\end{equation}
in the Loewner order, and consequently
\begin{equation}
  \bb^\top\Sh(\mu)^{-1}\bb\le \rf^{-1}\,\bb^\top\Mh^{-1}\bb
  \qquad\text{for every }\bb\in\R^{N_h}.
  \label{eq:mass-upper}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:periodisation} and Plancherel's theorem,
\[
  \zz^\top\Sh\zz=a_\mu(z_h,z_h)=a_\R(\widetilde z_h,\widetilde z_h)
  =\frac1{2\pi}\int_\R\Real A_\mu(\xi)\,\bigl|\widehat{\widetilde z_h}(\xi)\bigr|^2\,d\xi .
\]
The exact symbol identity of \cite{DamircheliCGMY2026}, namely
\begin{equation}
\Real A_\mu(\xi)
=\rf+C\Gamma(-Y)\Bigl[\bigl(M^Y-\Real(M-i\xi)^Y\bigr)
+\bigl(G^Y-\Real(G+i\xi)^Y\bigr)\Bigr],
\label{eq:symbol-real-exact}
\end{equation}
together with $\Gamma(-Y)>0$ for $Y\in(1,2)$ and the monotonicity
$\Real(a\pm i\xi)^Y\le a^Y$, gives $\Real A_\mu(\xi)\ge \rf$ for all $\xi$.  Hence
\[
  \zz^\top\Sh\zz\ \ge\ \rf\norm{\widetilde z_h}^2_{L^2(\R)}
  =\rf\norm{z_h}^2_{L^2(\Omega)}=\rf\,\zz^\top\Mh\zz .
\]
Inversion of the Loewner
inequality between positive definite matrices reverses it, giving
\eqref{eq:mass-upper}.
\end{proof}
Exact assembly also implies the mesh-uniform bound on $\kappa_h$:
\[
 \|\Ah v\|_{\Sh^{-1}}
 =\sup_{w\ne0}\frac{a_\mu(v_h,w_h)}{\|w\|_{\Sh}}
 \le\frac{C_{\rm cont}}{c_1}\|v\|_{\Sh}.
\]
For any assembled matrix with SPD symmetric part, duality gives
$\|\Ah v\|_{\Sh^{-1}}\ge v^\top\Ah v/\|v\|_{\Sh}=\|v\|_{\Sh}$,
proving $\kappa_h\ge1$. This quantity is an operator norm, rather than the
condition number of $\Sh$.
\begin{proposition}[Conservatism of a full-space dual norm]
\label{prop:full-space-conservative}
Suppose $\bar S\succ0$, and the reference and candidate have identical
prescribed coefficients. Then $S_I\succ0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \rho_I^\top S_I^{-1}\rho_I
 \le (E\rho_I)^\top\bar S^{-1}(E\rho_I).
 \label{eq:full-space-conservative}
\end{equation}
Consequently a valid upper Riesz bracket for the zero-padded free residual
using $\bar S$ is also a conservative reliability bound for the constrained
problem, subject to the same initial-error and arithmetic assumptions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The dual quadratic form equals
$\sup_v(2r^\top v-v^\top S v)$. Restricting the full-space supremum to
$v=Ew$ yields the free-space supremum, proving
\eqref{eq:full-space-conservative}. Positive definiteness restricts to every
principal subspace.
\end{proof}
The converse positivity implication fails because prescribed directions
can make $\bar S$ indefinite while $S_I$ is positive. The entire residual,
Riesz action and spectral bound must use the same constrained space.

\subsection{Discrete parameter stability}
\label{ssec:solution-operator}
\begin{proposition}[Discrete parameter stability of the solution map]
\label{prop:discrete-lipschitz}
Fix a mesh, a time grid, and a payoff $\psi$; the assembled symmetric
matrix $\Sh(\mu_1)$ is positive definite by Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral}. Let
$\mu_1,\mu_2\in\Pset$
with $\cc_h(\mu_1),\cc_h(\mu_2)$ the corresponding Crank--Nicolson
trajectories of \eqref{eq:cn-reference}.  Then, for every
$m=1,\dots,N_T$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \norm{\cc_h^m(\mu_1)-\cc_h^m(\mu_2)}_{\Mh}^2
  \le{}& \norm{\cc_h^0(\mu_1)-\cc_h^0(\mu_2)}_{\Mh}^2\\
  &+\dt\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}
  \bigl\lVert\bm\varrho^{\,n+1/2}\bigr\rVert_{\Sh(\mu_1)^{-1}}^2 ,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:discrete-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
where the parameter-perturbation residual is
\begin{equation}
  \bm\varrho^{\,n+1/2}
  :=\bigl(\Ah(\mu_1)-\Ah(\mu_2)\bigr)\overline\cc_h^{\,n+1/2}(\mu_2)
   -\bigl(\overline\ff^{\,n+1/2}(\mu_1)-\overline\ff^{\,n+1/2}(\mu_2)\bigr).
  \label{eq:param-residual}
\end{equation}
Under exact assembly of the conforming variational matrices,
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform-coercivity} additionally gives
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\bigl(\Ah(\mu_1)-\Ah(\mu_2)\bigr)\vv}_{\Sh(\mu_1)^{-1}}
  \le
  \frac{1}{c_1}\,
  \sup_{0\ne v_h,w_h\in\Vh}
  \frac{|a_{\mu_1}(v_h,w_h)-a_{\mu_2}(v_h,w_h)|}
       {\norm{v_h}_{\Vp{\mu_1}}\norm{w_h}_{\Vp{\mu_1}}}\;
  \norm{\vv}_{\Sh(\mu_1)},
  \label{eq:matrix-difference}
\end{equation}
so a bound on the difference of the two bilinear forms in a \emph{single}
norm---that of $\mu_1$---suffices, and no common energy space is needed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The difference $\ee^n:=\cc_h^n(\mu_1)-\cc_h^n(\mu_2)$ satisfies the
Crank--Nicolson error equation \eqref{eq:discrete-error-equation} with
$\rr_\theta^{n+1/2}$ replaced by $\bm\varrho^{\,n+1/2}$ of
\eqref{eq:param-residual}, since $\cc_h(\mu_2)$ solves the $\mu_2$ scheme
exactly.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate} with
$\Sh=\Sh(\mu_1)$.  Estimate \eqref{eq:matrix-difference} follows from
the continuity argument above applied to the bilinear form
$a_{\mu_1}-a_{\mu_2}$.
\end{proof}
This fixed-grid statement compares both trajectories in the norm of the
first parameter. A continuum regularity result across varying $Y$ would
require additional control of the changing energy spaces.

\subsection{Other time schemes and exponential shifts}
\label{ssec:semidiscrete}\label{ssec:be-certificate}\label{ssec:shifted}
The following extensions follow the same energy argument as
Theorem~\ref{thm:fully-discrete-certificate}.
For a differentiable candidate and a semidiscrete reference, define
$\rho=\Mh\dot\cc_\theta+\Ah\cc_\theta-\ff$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \|\ee(t)\|_{\Mh}^2+\int_0^t\|\ee\|_{\Sh}^2
 \le\|\ee(0)\|_{\Mh}^2+\int_0^t\|\rho\|_{\Sh^{-1}}^2.
 \label{eq:semidiscrete-certificate}
\end{equation}
Multiply the error equation by $\ee^\top$, apply Young's inequality, and
integrate.
For backward Euler, the residual is
$\rho^{n+1}=\Mh(c_\theta^{n+1}-c_\theta^n)/\dt_n
+\Ah c_\theta^{n+1}-f^{n+1}$ and
\begin{equation}
 \|\ee^{n+1}\|_{\Mh}^2+\|\ee^{n+1}-\ee^n\|_{\Mh}^2
 +\dt_n\|\ee^{n+1}\|_{\Sh}^2
 \le\|\ee^n\|_{\Mh}^2+\dt_n\|\rho^{n+1}\|_{\Sh^{-1}}^2.
 \label{eq:be-certificate}
\end{equation}
This follows from
$2y^\top\Mh(y-z)=\|y\|_{\Mh}^2-\|z\|_{\Mh}^2+\|y-z\|_{\Mh}^2$.
A Rannacher start combines backward-Euler half steps with CN
\cite{Rannacher1984}; summing the corresponding bounds, with the initial
term retained, gives the certificate for the mixed trajectory.
Each step must use its own scheme's residual.

For an exact-assembly G\aa rding bound
$a_\mu(v,v)\ge c_1\|v\|_{V_\mu}^2-c_2\|v\|_{L^2}^2$, choose
$\sigma>c_2$ and set
$A_\sigma=\Ah+\sigma\Mh$, $S_\sigma=\Sh+\sigma\Mh$.
Then $S_\sigma\succeq(\sigma-c_2)\Mh$.
Advance $d_h$ by CN for the shifted operator with forcing
$g^n=e^{-\sigma\tau_n}f^n$, and reconstruct
$c_{h,\sigma}^n=e^{\sigma\tau_n}d_h^n$.
For $d_\theta^n=e^{-\sigma\tau_n}c_\theta^n$ and its shifted CN residual
$\rho_\sigma$, the same theorem yields
\[
 \max_m\|c_{h,\sigma}^m-c_\theta^m\|_{\Mh}
 \le e^{\sigma T}
 \left(\|d_h^0-d_\theta^0\|_{\Mh}^2+
 \dt\sum_n\|\rho_\sigma^{n+1/2}\|_{S_\sigma^{-1}}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
This certifies the reconstructed shifted target. Exponential transformation
and CN discretisation do not commute, so it is a different discrete
trajectory from unshifted CN. The numerical studies use unshifted CN with
positive rates.

\subsection{Derivative bounds and continuum error separation}
\label{ssec:derivatives}\label{ssec:total-error}
For an $H^2(\Omega)$ spline space (cardinal order $p\ge3$), define
$R_h^{(j)}=((\phi_{h,k'}^{(j)},\phi_{h,k}^{(j)}))$ and
$\Lambda_j=\lambda_{\max}(R_h^{(j)},\Mh)$, $j=1,2$.
Since the common lifting cancels,
\[
 \|\partial_x^j(V_h^m-V_{\theta,h}^m)\|_{L^2}
 \le\sqrt{\Lambda_j}\eta_{\rm aug},
 \qquad \Lambda_j=O(h^{-2j}).
\]
Indeed, $\ee^\top R_h^{(j)}\ee\le\Lambda_j\ee^\top\Mh\ee$.
With the Greeks $\Delta=e^{-x}V_x$ and $\Gamma_{\rm opt}=e^{-2x}(V_{xx}-V_x)$,
\[
 \|\Delta_h-\Delta_\theta\|_{L^2}
 \le e^{-x_{\min}}\sqrt{\Lambda_1}\eta_{\rm aug},\qquad
 \|\Gamma_{{\rm opt},h}-\Gamma_{{\rm opt},\theta}\|_{L^2}
 \le e^{-2x_{\min}}(\sqrt{\Lambda_2}+\sqrt{\Lambda_1})\eta_{\rm aug}.
\]
These global factors can be conservative on a wide log-price domain and
grow under refinement. A rigorous numerical supremum of the point factor
in \eqref{eq:gamma-infty} requires validated cellwise extrema.

Under exact assembly, the regularity and buffer assumptions of
\cite{DamircheliCGMY2026}, and an identical prescribed smooth lifting,
\begin{equation}
 \max_n\|V(\tau_n)-V_h^n\|_{L^2(\Omega)}
 \le C_\mu(h^{p-Y/2}+\dt^2+\varepsilon_b).
 \label{eq:deterministic-error}
\end{equation}
The lifting discrepancies vanish because it is prescribed identically.
Combining this estimate with \eqref{eq:total-error-split} gives
\[
 \|V(\tau_m)-V_{\theta,h}^m\|_{L^2}
 \le C_\mu(h^{p-Y/2}+\dt^2+\varepsilon_b)+\eta_{\rm aug}.
\]
The deterministic constants and buffer error are not evaluated as
computable bounds here; the first term is assessed instead by the price comparison of
Section~\ref{ssec:calibration}, and the certificate supplies the second.

\subsection{Preconditioning and correction}
\label{ssec:correction-extension}
If $P_h\succ0$ and $\Sh\succeq\gamma_P P_h$ with $\gamma_P>0$, then
$r^\top\Sh^{-1}r\le\gamma_P^{-1}r^\top P_h^{-1}r$.
Alternatively $P_h$ can accelerate the Riesz iteration while the bracket
uses its independent bound on $\lambda_{\min}(\Sh)$.
\begin{proposition}[Contraction of a preconditioned correction]
\label{prop:correction-contraction}
Fix a time step and write the step equation as $B_+\zz=\bb$.  Let $P\succ0$ be
a preconditioner and consider the Richardson iteration
$\zz^{(i+1)}=\zz^{(i)}+P^{-1}(\bb-B_+\zz^{(i)})$ with residuals
$\sres^{(i)}=\bb-B_+\zz^{(i)}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\sres^{(i+1)}}_{P^{-1}}\le\rho_P\,\norm{\sres^{(i)}}_{P^{-1}},
  \qquad
  \rho_P:=\bigl\lVert I-P^{-1/2}B_+P^{-1/2}\bigr\rVert_2 .
  \label{eq:contraction}
\end{equation}
If $P=\Mh+\tfrac{\dt}{2}\Sh$, the symmetric part of $B_+$, then
$I-P^{-1/2}B_+P^{-1/2}=-\tfrac{\dt}{2}P^{-1/2}N_hP^{-1/2}$ with
$N_h=\tfrac12(\Ah-\Ah^\top)$ skew symmetric, so $\rho_P$ is determined
entirely by the nonsymmetric part of the CGMY operator and satisfies
$\rho_P\le\tfrac{\dt}{2}\lVert P^{-1/2}N_hP^{-1/2}\rVert_2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\sres^{(i+1)}=(I-B_+P^{-1})\sres^{(i)}$, so
$P^{-1/2}\sres^{(i+1)}=(I-P^{-1/2}B_+P^{-1/2})P^{-1/2}\sres^{(i)}$ and
\eqref{eq:contraction} follows by taking Euclidean norms.  For the second
claim, $B_+=P+\tfrac{\dt}{2}N_h$ by \eqref{eq:Sh} and the definition of $N_h$.
\end{proof}
The proposition proves contraction only when $\rho_P<1$; it is a
Richardson estimate and makes no contraction or timing assertion about the
GMRES correction used in the numerical experiments.
